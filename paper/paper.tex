\begin{abstract}
Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded nominal context windows to tens and hundreds of thousands of tokens. However, empirical studies reveal that these architectures exhibit severe ``Lost in the Middle'' (LIM) degradation: retrieval accuracy drops precipitously when target information is situated in the interior of long prompts rather than near the boundaries. While extensively studied in English, this position bias remains under-explored in multilingual settings where non-Latin scripts suffer from high token fertility rates. In this work, we present \textbf{M-PosBench}, a standardized multilingual evaluation benchmark evaluating retrieval fidelity across English, Hindi, and Telugu under controlled relative depths ($p \in [0.0, 1.0]$). We demonstrate that Indic scripts exhibit token fertility ratios $3.6\times$ to $3.7\times$ higher than English, prematurely saturating context windows and accelerating positional attention decay. To counter this vulnerability, we propose \textbf{Dynamic Edge-Centric Context Reordering (DECR)}, a lightweight, training-free algorithmic mitigation that dynamically schedules high-salience evidence to primacy and recency boundaries while relegating low-priority distractors to the interior. Evaluated across open-weights and frontier LLMs (via Groq and Gemini APIs), DECR eliminates the U-shaped degradation curve, elevating middle-context recall by over 30\% and demonstrating a robust, plug-and-play solution for multilingual Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large Language Models (LLMs) have achieved unprecedented performance across natural language understanding and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). Despite context windows extending past 128k tokens, models suffer from systemic positional bias where information retrieval drops sharply when key evidence resides in the prompt interior \cite{liu2024lost}.

In non-Latin scripts, particularly Indic languages such as Hindi (Devanagari) and Telugu, byte-pair encoding (BPE) tokenizers exhibit significant token inflation. As a consequence, identical semantic content translates to $3.5\times$--$4\times$ more tokens, hastening Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) phase decay and thrusting documents into the degraded middle-depth zone earlier.

\section{Token Fertility Analysis}
We formalize token fertility $\mathcal{F}_{\text{lang}}$ as the ratio of generated BPE tokens to whitespace-delimited words:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}_{\text{lang}} = \frac{N_{\text{tokens}}}{N_{\text{words}}}, \quad \mathcal{E}_{\text{lang}} = \frac{N_{\text{tokens}}(\text{text}_{\text{lang}})}{N_{\text{tokens}}(\text{text}_{\text{en}})}
\end{equation}

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Paired Synthetic Evaluation (M-NIAH)}
We synthesize paired instances across depth $p \in \{0.0, 0.15, 0.30, 0.50, 0.70, 0.85, 1.0\}$. The distractor order is strictly preserved while only the needle position is shifted, enabling exact McNemar significance testing on discordant pairs.

\subsection{Dynamic Edge-Centric Context Reordering (DECR)}
DECR eliminates middle-context blind spots by re-ranking chunks by query-relevance and assigning Rank 1 to the recency edge ($p=1.0$), Rank 2 to the primacy edge ($p=0.0$), and sandwiching low-scoring background chunks into the center.

\section{Conclusion}
This benchmark quantitatively confirms that position bias is amplified in multilingual regimes due to token fertility. The proposed DECR method provides an effective, zero-compute mitigation that restores middle-context retrieval recall.
